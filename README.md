## FAIR in a nutshell

| Principle | Key question to ask about your data |
|-----------|-------------------------------------|
| **F**indable | Does it have a persistent identifier (e.g. a DOI) and rich metadata that is indexed somewhere searchable? |
| **A**ccessible | Can people (and machines) retrieve it through a standard protocol, and is the metadata available even if the data itself is restricted? |
| **I**nteroperable | Does it use open, community-standard formats and controlled vocabularies? |
| **R**eusable | Does it have a clear license, provenance, and enough documentation for someone else to understand and reuse it? |

> FAIR does not mean "open". Data can be FAIR while access is restricted — what matters is that the conditions for access and reuse are clear.

## Next steps after the workshop

- **Start a Data Management Plan** for your current project — even a short one helps. Check whether your funder or institute provides a template.
- **Pick a trusted repository** for your data before you need it, so you know which formats and metadata it expects.
- **Add a README and a license** to every dataset you share.
- **Use persistent identifiers** for yourself ([ORCID](https://orcid.org/)), your data (DOI), and your institution ([ROR](https://ror.org/)).
- **Ask for help** — your institute's data management or library support team is there for exactly this.
